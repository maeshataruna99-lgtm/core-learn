### Method overview

The study compared a paper-based manual control condition (CG) with four AR conditions formed by crossing information mode (video vs. 3D animation) and interaction mode (hand gesture vs. voice command): VH, VV, AH, and AV. Participants performed a gearbox maintenance task (replace a non-working brake) with a Microsoft HoloLens 2 running Vuforia 8.3.8 and Unity 2019.1.10f; AR instructions were always-on. Outcomes included completion time and errors, NASA-TLX workload, Empatica E4 EDA/IBI-derived arousal and HRV (RMSSD), PSSUQ usability, subjective questions, and HoloLens eye-tracking metrics (dwell time, fixation count, transitions, fixation rate). After consent, baseline physiology, HMD fitting, eye-tracker calibration, and training/familiarisation, participants completed the task in their assigned condition (pp. 6–8).

## 4. Method

### Method: task procedure and questionnaires

The maintenance task was a disassembly/assembly task using bare hands and Allen keys. Participants could not interact with the researcher unless a technical problem occurred. After the task, participants completed questionnaires on perceived workload and usability; control-group participants then viewed and ran through the AR maintenance tasks without doing the task and rated workload/usability relative to the paper manual.

> Source: p. 9: “The maintenance task involved a disassembly and assembly process using the combination of bare hands and some Allen keys. During the experiment, the participant was not allowed to interact with the researcher unless they encountered a technical problem.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 9: “Upon completing the task, the participant was asked to fill out the questionnaires to assess their subjective perception toward the task and the method in accomplishing the task in terms of perceived workload and usability.” _(excerpt verified in source)_

## 4.4. Data analysis

### Method: statistical analysis

Analysis used one-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD; log transformation or Kruskal-Wallis for non-normal data; two-way ANOVA for information-by-interaction effects; Mann-Whitney U for non-normal main effects; repeated-measures/mixed ANOVA for physiological and eye-tracking data; z-score standardisation; Spearman correlation; and SPSS v26. Significance was p < 0.05, with Bonferroni correction where applicable.

## 6. Discussion

**Objective (p. 14).** The study investigated how different AR modalities affect manufacturing and maintenance work with AR head-mounted displays, specifically comparing information modes (video vs. 3D animation) and interaction modes (hand gesture vs. voice command).

> Source: p. 14: “The purpose of this study was to investigate the impact of different AR modalities concerning commonly used types of information mode (i. e. video vs. 3D animation) and interaction modes (i.e. hand gesture and voice command) for AR HMD systems in manufacturing and maintenance applications.” _(excerpt verified in source)_

### 6.6. Limitations

**Method (as reported on pp. 13–16).** Participants used AR HMD instructions (Microsoft Hololens 2) for maintenance assembly/disassembly involving an Allen key and simple hand movements. The study compared video vs. 3D animation and hand gesture vs. voice command, with a paper-based manual as the non-AR comparison. The tested tasks were relatively simple and limited, and the authors note that the duration/environment may not reflect a noisy, poorly lit industrial setting.

> Source: p. 16: “Firstly, the maintenance tasks tested in this study were relatively simple and limited, consisting of the use of an Allen key to (un)screw bolts and simple hand movements to perform the assembly and disassembly process.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 15: “this study also found that the number of task errors was significantly reduced when using AR compared to paper-based manuals but only for the 3D animation-based visualisation.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 14: “since they were using it only for 7–8 min on average, most of them questioned if it would be comfortable for prolonged use.” _(excerpt verified in source)_

### 6.1. How is AR task performance affected by different types of information mode (i.e. video vs. 3D animation) and interaction mode (i.e. hand gesture vs. voice command)?

**Task performance (pp. 14–15).** All AR conditions significantly improved completion time versus the paper-based manual, and 3D animation instructions were 14% faster than video. The study found no significant performance gain between hand gesture and voice command; task errors were significantly reduced with AR versus paper only for 3D animation, and video-based instructions produced significantly more mistakes than 3D animation.

> Source: p. 14: “The findings show that although all AR conditions significantly improved task performance in terms of completion time over a traditional paper-based manual, the 3D animation-based instruction show a significantly faster completion time (14%) compared to a video-based visualisation.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 15: “this study also found that the number of task errors was significantly reduced when using AR compared to paper-based manuals but only for the 3D animation-based visualisation.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 15: “It was also found that participants who used video-based instructions made significantly more mistakes than those who experienced 3D animation-based instructions.” _(excerpt verified in source)_  
> Source: p. 15: “Taken all this, this study found that the interaction mode did not affect the performance gain whereas type of information mode caused variations in the task performance (i.e. time completion and accuracy).” _(excerpt verified in source)_

### 6.2. Do AR instructions lead to lower workload than a paper-based manual?

**Workload/physiology (p. 15).** Overall subjective workload did not show a statistically significant difference, but within-subject comparison for the paper-based manual showed significant differences for Mental, Physical, Effort, and Total workload after the AR experience.
